# Direction 02 — Pixel Grammar

Status: reversible challenger; not selected.

## Concept thesis

> **Turn the avatar's pixel construction into a mature identity grammar.**

The green pixel mark is currently a palette and corner motif. This direction
extracts one genuine geometric rule—cut, step, proportion, alignment, or
negative space—and uses it to bind name, thesis, and work.

It is not pixel art, a retro-game theme, a mascot system, or a HUD.

## Cooper-specific signature

Develop one **Cooper Cut** or **Cooper Glyph** from the avatar's exact geometry:

- trace a stable ratio or stepped negative-space cut;
- make it participate in the name or thesis rather than sit in a corner;
- use one green solid, one neutral field, and mature non-pixel typography;
- repeat the rule at most once outside the core glyph;
- prohibit random block clusters, icons, sprites, game frames, maps, inventory,
  health bars, terminal chrome, CRT effects, and scan lines.

This is the direction with the strongest need for a professional identity
designer. An arbitrary blocky `CO` mark would fail.

## Positioning problem solved

v1 contains an avatar-derived visual but it is not causally connected to the
content. Pixel Grammar makes the public avatar an unmistakable signature while
the positioning explains the professional continuity:

> From Agent Skills and workflow control planes to released software and tested
> systems, Cooper makes work another person can inspect.

The form supplies recognition. The proof map supplies credibility.

## Content-to-visual grammar

The geometric rule expresses three scales of intervention without becoming a
taxonomy grid:

- `SKILL` — shapes how a person starts work with an Agent;
- `LOOP` — shapes how an AI-assisted workflow retains state and gates;
- `SHIP` — shapes how a usable artifact is released, supported, or tested.

These are conceptual lenses, not a claim that three repositories form one
runtime. They should remain open text, never contained chips or equal cards.

The native Profile still uses one outcome sentence and direct proof for each
selected project. The glyph is not repeated beside every heading.

## Candidate signature language

One possible hierarchy:

```text
[COOPER GLYPH]  COOPER OAK
                AI-NATIVE WORKFLOW BUILDER
                SKILLS / CONTROL PLANES / PROOF
```

The role label remains a candidate. A more evidence-safe version is:

```text
[COOPER GLYPH]  COOPER OAK
                MAKING AI-ASSISTED WORK INSPECTABLE
```

The final copy cannot be chosen by the glyph exercise.

## Desktop composition

- A 1200 × 280 shallow, asymmetric field is a starting envelope, not a spec.
- The glyph occupies roughly 25–32% as the only graphic form.
- Name and thesis occupy the remaining field with deliberate negative space.
- The glyph and type overlap, cut, or align according to one explicit rule.
- Avoid v1's “large left text plus small corner decoration” arrangement.
- No pixel font; system sans or a specialist-approved type treatment carries
  professional tone.
- A real token such as `/goal` may appear only if clearly tied to the work; it
  must not be shown inside a prompt box or terminal.

## 390px composition

- Recompose the glyph above or beside a two-line name; do not shrink it into an
  icon corner.
- Limit the signature to glyph, name, and one thesis.
- Convert horizontal categories to two or three open text lines.
- Remove secondary metadata and long repository names from the SVG.
- Keep `/goal` as typography only if used—no cursor, shell, input, or button.
- Verify the negative-space cut remains recognizable at 320px and 390px.

## Light and dark rules

- `#6ed959` remains the single identity color.
- Light: pale neutral field, near-black type, green solid glyph.
- Dark: charcoal field, near-white type, green solid glyph.
- No glow, bevel, shadow, gradient, glass, texture, or pseudo-depth.
- Rebalance negative space and stroke/field weight for dark rather than simply
  inverting colors.
- The name and thesis remain legible if forced-color mode removes the green.

## GitHub/GFM and generator constraints

- Four local SVG assets remain appropriate: desktop/mobile × light/dark.
- Prefer a small number of basic path/rect primitives.
- No external font or remote image. If specialist lettering is converted to
  paths, retain a documented editable source and licensing record.
- Native Markdown must carry the full name, thesis, project evidence, and links.
- Do not distribute square glyph bullets or block emoji through Markdown; GFM
  cannot sustain a full grid system and repeated motifs will become toy-like.
- A selected implementation needs a new theme schema and generator logic, not
  a new asset dropped into v1's fixed composition.

## AI claim-to-proof mapping

The most credible AI token is `/goal`, linked to the installable `goal-to-do`
Skill. It may participate in the grammar if native text immediately explains
its meaning and installation proof.

The workflow harness and preflight protocol can support `LOOP` or `GATE` in
native copy with experimental/prototype labels. Pixel geometry itself must not
act as “AI futurism.” No robot, node network, neural texture, model logo, chat
bubble, or prompt-window decoration is allowed.

## Accessibility

- The glyph is decorative identity, not the sole carrier of the name or claim.
- Native text repeats all important information.
- Do not use a pixel font for sentences, Chinese text, proof labels, or small
  metadata.
- Test font fallback, especially English/Chinese combinations and repository
  names.
- Verify AA contrast, 200% zoom, 320px, 390px, light/dark, and image failure.
- The stepped cut cannot be the only way content categories differ.

## Swap test

This direction passes only when:

- the glyph can be reconstructed from Cooper's actual avatar geometry;
- its cut/proportion rule affects the wordmark or thesis composition;
- `/goal` or another real Cooper artifact supplies content-specific meaning;
- only one glyph and one green exist;
- the result would not fit a generic pixel-avatar developer without redesign.

It fails immediately if the concept depends on pixel fonts, an arbitrary `CO`,
random blocks, Minecraft/arcade associations, terminal chrome, or a game UI.

## Failure risks

### Toy and game regression

Risk: the strongest visual association becomes 8-bit games rather than AI-native
workflow rigor.
Control: mature typography, extreme motif restraint, and no representational
game symbols.

### Temporary-logo quality

Risk: an unrefined block glyph looks like a placeholder app icon.
Control: do not implement without specialist proportion, counterform, optical
weight, and responsive reduction work.

### Form outruns proof

Risk: the Profile is memorable but the AI thesis is still vague.
Control: native positioning and direct install/prototype evidence remain above
or immediately after the signature.

### Motif saturation

Risk: repeating the cut across headings, bullets, and project separators creates
plastic components.
Control: one core glyph, at most one secondary rule, no component kit.

## Difference from the other directions

- Unlike Proof as Identity, recognizable geometry leads and proof supports it.
- Unlike Public Field Notes, it does not require personal narrative.
- It offers the greatest visual ownership and the highest craft risk.

## Recommendation and reversal

Advance only if the user's priority is unmistakable visual identity and a
qualified provider is available. Reverse if:

- the glyph cannot pass the avatar traceability test;
- professional review finds a retro-game or app-logo reading;
- the AI thesis becomes secondary to pixel style;
- mobile reduction destroys the negative-space rule.

No glyph should be drafted into production assets under route revision-001.
