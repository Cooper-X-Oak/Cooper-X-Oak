# Direction 03 — Public Field Notes

Status: reversible challenger; not selected; requires first-person input.

## Concept thesis

> **The work leaves a usable trail.**

Building in public becomes more than a date or maker slogan. The Profile shows
that Cooper publishes the route another person can follow: installation,
boundaries, tests, release notes, recovery, and what changed along the way.

This is an editorial field record, not simulated paper, a notebook UI, or a
blog index.

## Cooper-specific signature

Use one **Field Margin**: an avatar-derived stepped edge that marks authorship
without enclosing content. Pair it with one editorial sentence and Cooper's
name.

Possible statement:

```text
THE WORK
LEAVES A TRAIL.
```

Possible native support:

> I build in public so the work leaves a route someone else can install, run,
> inspect, or verify.

Prohibited authenticity props:

- fake dates, coordinates, issue numbers, revision stamps, or “live” status;
- paper texture, tape, torn edges, notebook holes, sticky notes, handwriting,
  rubber stamps, or card shadows;
- invented quotations, annotations, lessons, or diary entries.

## Positioning problem solved

v1's `BUILT IN PUBLIC · 2026—` is metadata. This direction turns public work
into an authorial practice: not merely showing outcomes, but leaving setup,
support boundaries, tests, and honest maturity for someone else.

It can make Cooper feel more human and less like a technical system. It cannot
become specific enough without first-person material from Cooper.

## Content-to-visual grammar

The native page uses three short field records, not product cards:

1. what Cooper was trying to make;
2. what another person can install, run, or inspect now;
3. one honest boundary or maturity label;
4. direct proof;
5. optionally, one first-person decision or lesson supplied by Cooper.

Candidate records:

- `goal-to-do`: from vague long-running work to an installable alignment Skill;
- `writing-loop-harness` or `loop-preflight`: why workflow state and gates need
  to survive outside a chat;
- `LongYinMod_RisingFame`: what shipping, setup, troubleshooting, and support
  taught about user-facing boundaries;
- `valvetrade-pipeline`: how tests make database assumptions inspectable.

Do not invent the motivation or lesson from code. If Cooper supplies none, omit
that layer and acknowledge the reduced authorship.

## Required first-person inputs

Before selection, request at least one factual sentence for each chosen record:

- What ambiguity or pain started the project?
- What constraint changed the design?
- What did shipping or testing expose?
- What boundary would Cooper keep next time?

These sentences are content authority. A design provider may edit for clarity
but cannot manufacture them.

## Desktop composition

- Start within a shallow 1200 × 280 envelope.
- Allocate roughly 15–20% to a stepped green margin and author signature.
- Use the remaining field for one large sentence and a concise scope line.
- No three-note panels, paper columns, faux publication metadata, or content
  previews inside the image.
- A stable serif/sans relationship may be explored only with careful system
  fallback and bilingual testing.
- The native first field record should remain near the first viewport.

## 390px composition

- Collapse the side margin into a short top or bottom step.
- Break the main sentence across three or four deliberate lines.
- Keep only name, thesis, and one scope line in the image.
- Move all annotations and project specifics into native Markdown.
- Preserve a single column at 390px and 320px; no table, float, or note stack.

## Light and dark rules

- Use clean neutral GitHub-adjacent fields, not cream paper simulation.
- Keep avatar green as the only identity accent.
- Light: near-white/pale neutral, near-black text.
- Dark: charcoal, near-white text.
- Do not use low-contrast gray to imitate pencil or marginalia.
- No texture, transparency stack, shadow, gradient, glow, or paper depth.
- Check English/Chinese font fallback in both themes.

## GitHub/GFM and generator constraints

- Four local responsive/theme SVG variants remain appropriate.
- Field records themselves are normal Markdown headings, paragraphs, and links.
- No HTML table for marginal notes; narrow reflow and accessibility are too
  fragile.
- No CSS columns, floats, absolute annotations, or footnote indices that look
  interactive.
- Current notes must not create a high-churn promise. Prefer durable project
  decisions and boundaries over “this week” or “currently building” copy.
- Update the structured authoring source and regenerate; never hand-edit README.

## AI claim-to-proof mapping

The first record should be a real AI-native artifact:

- `goal-to-do` supplies release and install proof;
- `writing-loop-harness` supplies experimental file-native control-plane proof;
- `loop-preflight` supplies tested gate/preflight proof;
- `awesome-ai-native-os` can appear only as a taxonomy draft.

`LongYinMod_RisingFame` and `valvetrade-pipeline` demonstrate release and test
discipline. They must not be relabeled as AI work.

Do not call the page an `AI lab`, `research journal`, or completed handbook
without corresponding published research/evidence.

## Accessibility

- Native text contains the complete claim and records; the image is a signature.
- Candidate alt: `Cooper Oak profile signature: the work leaves a usable trail.`
- Avoid fine gray text, handwriting, or small marginal labels.
- Test AA contrast, bilingual fallback, 200% zoom, 320px, 390px, light/dark, and
  image failure.
- The stepped margin is decorative and never the sole hierarchy cue.
- Links remain ordinary GitHub anchors with explicit destinations.

## Swap test

This direction currently has the weakest swap-test result because “field notes”
and “build in public” can fit any indie maker.

It passes only after it contains:

- Cooper's actual English/Chinese voice;
- real project motives or decisions supplied by Cooper;
- the exact span from installable Agent Skill to released mod and tested system;
- specific setup, boundary, test, or support trails;
- the avatar-derived stepped margin.

Without those, `PUBLIC FIELD NOTES · 2026` is more styled than v1 but no more
authored.

## Failure risks

### Generic editorial template

Risk: a tasteful magazine layout substitutes for a personal brand.
Control: require real Cooper-authored decisions and project-specific trails.

### Fabricated intimacy

Risk: the designer invents motives, lessons, dates, or annotations.
Control: no first-person statement without a direct user or public source.

### Blog sprawl

Risk: the Profile becomes a long chronological journal.
Control: three durable records, two short paragraphs each, one repository exit.

### AI dilution

Risk: warm process storytelling hides the current AI-native direction.
Control: lead with released/installable AI proof and keep maturity explicit.

### Maintenance decay

Risk: “current notes” become stale.
Control: document stable decisions and evidence, not dates or transient status.

## Difference from the other directions

- Unlike Proof as Identity, authorship and process lead while proof supports the
  narrative.
- Unlike Pixel Grammar, the avatar geometry is a quiet margin, not the identity
  centerpiece.
- It offers the strongest human voice but depends on missing content authority.

## Recommendation and reversal

Keep as a real alternative, but do not select until Cooper supplies first-person
material and confirms that a warmer editorial identity is preferable to direct
professional proof.

Reverse if:

- no genuine first-person material is available;
- the page begins to resemble paper, a blog index, or a note-card wall;
- the AI-native thesis becomes secondary;
- maintenance requires frequent status updates.

Even with first-person input, a professional art-direction provider and a new
sealed route revision remain required before implementation.
