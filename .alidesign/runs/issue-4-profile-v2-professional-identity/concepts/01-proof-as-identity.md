# Direction 01 — Proof as Identity

Status: reversible hypothesis; recommended for first review, not selected.

## Concept thesis

> **Proof is part of the build.**

Cooper's identity does not need a skills wall, senior title, dashboard, or
quantity claim. It can be established through a repeated behavior: make work
another person can install, release, run, inspect, or verify.

The direction translates that behavior into one editorial identity system. It
must never turn evidence into badges or self-issued status.

## Cooper-specific signature

Use one **Proof Spine**: a stepped line or narrow stepped field derived from the
avatar's real pixel-cut proportions. It is not a progress indicator and does
not connect fake workflow stages.

The spine relates three plain-text proof actions to real artifacts:

- `INSTALL` — `goal-to-do`;
- `RELEASE` — `LongYinMod_RisingFame`;
- `TEST` — `valvetrade-pipeline`.

`WRITABLE`, `LIVE`, `PUBLIC`, `PASSED`, colored dots, checkmarks, scores,
percentages, and timestamps are prohibited. The actual link or artifact is the
proof.

## Positioning problem solved

v1 says Cooper builds game mods, AI tools, and useful experiments. That is
truthful but categorical. This concept answers why a professional visitor
should care:

> Cooper makes AI-assisted work explicit and leaves proof that survives beyond
> the prompt.

The installable Codex Skill can lead the AI-native claim. The released mod and
tested pipeline then prove that release, support, and verification discipline
is not confined to Agent vocabulary.

## Content-to-visual grammar

The grammar is:

```text
ACTION THESIS
    ↕
REAL ARTIFACT — DIRECT PROOF
```

It is not drawn as a diagram. In the shallow signature, one large sentence and
three aligned proof verbs share the stepped spine. In native Markdown, every
selected project uses:

1. what was made;
2. honest maturity;
3. how another person can install, run, or inspect it;
4. one or two direct proof links.

Projects remain vertically ordered text. No three-column cards or equal product
tiles.

## Candidate signature language

Primary visual statement:

```text
PROOF IS PART
OF THE BUILD.
```

Native positioning immediately below the visual:

```text
I make AI-assisted work inspectable, repeatable, and shippable.
Agent skills, file-native workflow control planes, and proof-backed tools.
```

The final public sentence must be selected separately; this is composition
material, not accepted copy.

## Desktop composition

Target a shallow, wide signature around 1200 × 280, subject to later craft
review:

- roughly 58–62% for the method statement;
- roughly 38–42% for three proof lines;
- one stepped spine establishes the relationship without enclosing either side;
- `COOPER OAK` is a restrained author signature, not the only oversized text;
- the action thesis is the focal point;
- proof lines are large enough to remain legible in the rendered README;
- generous empty field carries professional weight;
- no outer card, rounded rectangle, chip, table, rule grid, or interface chrome.

The native H1/positioning and first meaningful link should remain visible near
the first viewport instead of being pushed below a decorative hero.

## 390px composition

The mobile asset is recomposed, not scaled:

```text
COOPER OAK

PROOF IS PART
OF THE BUILD.

INSTALL
RELEASE
TEST
```

- the proof spine becomes a short left step or a bottom step;
- repository names move to native Markdown to avoid small SVG text;
- verbs remain words, never color-only code;
- auxiliary metadata disappears;
- verify both 390px and 320px without clipping or compressed line lengths.

## Light and dark rules

- Keep avatar green `#6ed959` as the single identity color.
- Light uses a neutral GitHub-adjacent pale field and near-black type.
- Dark uses a GitHub-adjacent charcoal field and near-white type.
- No gradient, glow, blur, glass, shadow, filter, or texture.
- Dark is optically rebalanced rather than mechanically inverted; the green
  spine must not overpower the thesis.
- Proof actions remain understandable if the green is removed or forced colors
  replace it.

## GitHub/GFM and generator constraints

- Retain local `<picture>` sources for desktop/mobile and light/dark.
- Use basic SVG paths, rectangles, and text only; no script, animation,
  `foreignObject`, remote font, or external asset.
- Native Markdown repeats the identity and meaning; the image is never the sole
  source of the claim or proof.
- Proof links remain normal Markdown anchors outside the SVG.
- The v1 generator hard-codes theme and composition assumptions. A selected v2
  implementation must change the authoring schema and generator deliberately,
  not replace only banner strings.
- The image must remain shallow enough that GitHub-native content does the work.

## AI claim-to-proof mapping

The AI claim begins with `goal-to-do`, not abstract “AI systems” typography:

- claim: released installable Agent Skill;
- proof: direct Skill install path and `v0.1.0` release;
- supporting direction: experimental file-native harness and tested preflight;
- boundary: never imply adoption, a mature platform, or one integrated runtime.

`valvetrade-pipeline` proves system/test rigor and remains explicitly non-AI.
`LongYinMod_RisingFame` proves release and support. The concept must preserve
that division.

## Accessibility

- Candidate alt: `Cooper Oak profile signature: proof is part of the build.`
- Keep project details out of alt because native text immediately provides them.
- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast in both themes.
- Keep SVG text comfortably above v1's minimum rendered size.
- Test 320px, 390px, desktop, 200% zoom, light/dark, and image failure.
- INSTALL/RELEASE/TEST remain text, not color or line-position codes.
- Nothing resembles a button, selectable row, focus ring, toggle, checkbox, or
  command input.

## Swap test

This direction passes only if all of these remain necessary:

- the three proof verbs map to Cooper's real work;
- the spine proportions trace the existing avatar geometry;
- the AI proof is an actual installable Codex Skill;
- released-mod and tested-pipeline evidence show cross-domain delivery;
- the language uses Cooper's inspectable/evidence-gated method.

If the real projects are replaced with abstract `DISCOVER / BUILD / SHIP`, or the
spine becomes an arbitrary green line, the direction fails and becomes a
generic engineering consultancy poster.

## Failure risks

### Audit dashboard regression

Risk: proof becomes status UI, scored quality, ticks, badges, log rows, or an
evidence console.
Control: show direct proof nouns and links only; never score the author.

### Generic Swiss-tech poster

Risk: big sans text, a line, and uppercase labels can still be swapped to any
developer.
Control: real artifact/action relationships and avatar-specific geometry are
mandatory; arbitrary metadata is removed.

### Proof overwhelms personality

Risk: the profile feels legalistic or defensive.
Control: one confident thesis, restrained proof, and a brief human supporting
line; no compliance language.

## Difference from the other directions

- Unlike Pixel Grammar, identity originates in evidence behavior; the pixel
  motif supports rather than leads.
- Unlike Public Field Notes, it does not require invented or missing personal
  narrative.
- It makes the most direct professional and AI claim while staying closest to
  currently verifiable facts.

## Recommendation and reversal

Recommended for the first selection review. Reverse or narrow it if:

- the visitor/conversion values authorial process more than inspectable proof;
- Cooper rejects proof as a defining personal method;
- a professional reviewer cannot prevent the composition becoming audit UI;
- a different flagship project changes the strongest proof vocabulary.

Advancing this direction still requires a Cooper-specific art-direction
provider, explicit selection, IA comparison, craft criteria, and a new sealed
route revision.
