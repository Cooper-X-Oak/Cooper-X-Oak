# Profile v2 art-direction hypotheses

Guidance provider: `$review-design-quality`.

## Shared positioning basis

All three concepts begin from the same evidence-backed action thesis:

> **Making AI-assisted work inspectable, repeatable, and shippable.**

They may emphasize different dimensions, but none may upgrade experimental or
draft work into a mature platform, invent adoption, or turn independent
repositories into one fictional system.

## Direction A — Proof as Identity

**Thesis:** proof is part of the build.
**Signature:** a single avatar-derived stepped proof spine, one method statement,
and three real proof actions such as `INSTALL`, `RELEASE`, and `TEST`.
**Professional effect:** strongest connection between AI identity, delivery
discipline, and external evidence.
**Primary risk:** proof could regress into audit-dashboard labels, fake statuses,
or evidence cards.
**Provider dependency:** medium; typography and restraint are still professional
art-direction work.

## Direction B — Pixel Grammar

**Thesis:** the avatar's pixel geometry becomes an identity grammar rather than
corner decoration.
**Signature:** one Cooper-specific cut/glyph, a single scale/proportion rule, and
large mature typography.
**Professional effect:** highest visual ownership and strongest continuity with
the public avatar.
**Primary risk:** easily becomes an 8-bit game brand, HUD, temporary logo, or
plastic grid.
**Provider dependency:** high; glyph, proportion, negative space, and type
relationships cannot be solved by ordinary frontend styling.

## Direction C — Public Field Notes

**Thesis:** public work leaves a usable trail—install paths, boundaries, tests,
and lessons another person can follow.
**Signature:** a stepped field margin and authorial editorial sentence; no paper
simulation or note cards.
**Professional effect:** warmest and most personal articulation of building in
public.
**Primary risk:** without Cooper's first-person motivations, it becomes a
generic indie-maker magazine template.
**Provider dependency:** medium plus missing user-authored material.

## Comparative matrix

| Criterion | Proof as Identity | Pixel Grammar | Public Field Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Professional credibility | High: proof and maturity are explicit | High if glyph/type craft succeeds | High if first-person material is genuine |
| AI direction clarity | Highest: installable Skill and workflow proof lead | High: `/goal` or workflow token can anchor | Medium: story may dilute AI focus |
| Cooper-specific authorship | High through real proof vocabulary and avatar spine | Highest through a bespoke geometric grammar | Potentially high; currently depends on missing personal notes |
| GFM robustness | Highest; shallow signature plus native proof links | High; single SVG glyph requires careful fallback | Highest; native text carries most meaning |
| Narrow-screen risk | Low with a separate stacked mobile composition | Medium: glyph/type balance must be recomposed | Low if margin collapses to a short rule |
| Light/dark risk | Low; flat black/white/green | Medium; negative-space weight may change | Low; avoid low-contrast editorial gray |
| Plastic/toy regression | Medium if proof becomes badges/status UI | Highest if pixel becomes HUD or retro game styling | Low, but can become decorative pseudo-paper |
| Generic-template risk | Medium if proof language is abstract | Low if glyph is truly avatar-specific | Highest without first-person content |
| Maintenance | Low | Low after a stable glyph system exists | Medium; “current note” copy ages |
| Missing authority impact | Blocks final typography/composition | Strongly blocks glyph development | Blocks authorial copy and final editorial voice |

## Recommendation

Advance **Proof as Identity** to the first review because it transforms the
strongest existing fact—installable, releasable, testable proof—into brand
logic. It makes the AI position credible through `goal-to-do`, keeps delivery
discipline visible, survives GFM constraints, and creates the greatest
professional difference from v1 without requiring invented biography.

Keep **Pixel Grammar** as the deliberate challenger when the desired priority is
visual ownership. It should not advance without a specialist who can design the
glyph and typography without returning to the rejected game/HUD language.

Keep **Public Field Notes** as the human challenger. It should advance only if
Cooper supplies genuine motivations, decisions, or lessons for the selected
projects.
