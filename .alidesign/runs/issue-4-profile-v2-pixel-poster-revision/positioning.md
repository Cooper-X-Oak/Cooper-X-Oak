# Revision positioning

## Audience and first impression

Fellow builders and potential collaborators should first encounter a distinct
author identity, then verify the work through real GitHub evidence. The first
screen should say “this is Cooper Oak” through a mature, recognizable pixel Oak
monument—not through an abstract systems glyph, a developer dashboard, or a
wall of explanatory copy.

## Position

Cooper Oak publishes bounded experiments in keeping humans in control of
AI-assisted work. The visual identity should express rooted growth, deliberate
construction, and evidence accumulated over time. It should feel like an
independent digital poster and publishing system: confident, flat, exact, and
professional.

## Locked character

- Pixel visual poster, not pixel-game nostalgia.
- Monumental Oak, not a route diagram or ornamental logo.
- Extreme poster minimalism, not a dense control plane.
- Bone-white paper, near-black structure, forest-green body, and a single small
  signal-lime emphasis.
- Real GitHub facts and links below the first impression, not fake product UI.

## Rejection boundaries

- No plastic-green mascot, circular badge, sprite symmetry, gradients, glow,
  bevel, shadow, glass, or toy material.
- No terminal, HUD, dashboard, fake metric, fake status, or inactive control.
- No generic Markdown page with one decorative banner.
- No inaccessible poster that becomes the sole source of identity or proof.

## Success test

Without the name and without color, the primary silhouette is recognized as a
wide-canopy tree or Oak. Without images, the GFM still communicates identity,
proof, and action. With images, desktop and 320px views form one coherent,
authored poster rather than a document with attached decoration.
